\begin{abstract}
Two conditions decide whether a published FPGA arithmetic comparison means what
it appears to mean, and both are usually left unmeasured.

First, \textbf{synthesis deletes the checks whose area is being measured.}
On-die assertions are the standard defence against a design that routes and is
wrong, and the optimiser silently removes them: a census of twenty-eight clauses
across seven wrappers finds \textbf{fourteen folded to constants}, and a clause
that has become a constant reads PASS in every build, including the failing
ones. Repairing one wrapper so that its clauses survive raises it from $452$ to
$696$ LUT --- \textbf{thirty-five per cent of the routed design was absent from
the area published for it}. Two earlier verdicts change meaning in consequence:
a design reported as passing four of four clauses passed one of one actually
evaluated, and a design reported as failing two of four fails every clause it
evaluates. Marking cells \texttt{(* keep *)} does not fix this; making the
operands structurally distinct while carrying equal values does.

Second, \textbf{achieved frequency is largely a function of size.} Over eleven
designs placed and routed across five seeds each, spanning a $39.6\times$ range
of area, $F_{\max}=4397\cdot\mathrm{LUT}^{-0.648}$ with $R^{2}=0.920$; the
exponent is unchanged when the fit is taken over all fifty-five individual seed
points rather than the medians. A throughput-per-area ratio therefore divides a
quantity by a second that is already a function of it, and restates a ranking by
size --- on our own harness at a Spearman correlation of $0.961$ with ranking by
inverse area, while seed-to-seed spread reaches $20.5\%$ on a single design.

Neither result invalidates a frequency measurement; both invalidate figures
built from them. We then give six ways an evaluation reports success while
having failed, worked through a case in which every individual measurement is
correct and the comparison is not, and state which of our own published claims
each one withdraws.
\end{abstract}
