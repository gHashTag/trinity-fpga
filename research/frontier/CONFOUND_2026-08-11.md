# The row marked "not swept" was the most informative row in the table

`binary32`'s decoder is one line:

    assign fp32_out = binary32_in;

A wire, because `binary32` *is* `fp32`. Its conformance is exact by
construction, and **its 472 LUT are the harness alone** — LFSR, accumulator,
output fold, no decoder in them at all.

That number had never been taken, and it changes how the whole table reads.

## The harness is 80–102% of every measurement

Every row lies between 463 and 1,008 LUT against a harness floor of 472. For the
top ten rows the decoder contributes under 115 LUT — under a fifth of what is
being divided into.

**Proposition (harness dominance).** Reporting $R_f = M_f/(H+D_f)$ gives
$\partial R_f/\partial D_f = -M_f/(H+D_f)^2 \to -M_f/H^2$ as $D_f/H \to 0$. When
$H \gg D_f$ the report varies almost entirely with frequency and the decoder's
cost is compressed out of it.

## A format cannot be cheaper than a wire

`GFTernary` measures **463 LUT — nine fewer than the bare wire.** Alone against
a minimal register-and-fold sink: bare wire 112 LUT, `GFTernary` **66**.

**Proposition (output-space pruning).** A decoder with $n$ input bits has at
most $2^n$ distinct outputs; logic observing only those outputs may be
specialised to that image, so synthesised cost is non-increasing in $n$
independently of decoder complexity. Comparison across differing $n$ confounds
design with width.

Spearman correlation between storage width and MHz/LUT across the 21 rows:
**−0.455**. Width does not explain the ranking, but it is not absent from it
either, and two rows are explained by nothing else.

## Grouped by width, the table says something narrower and more defensible

Nine width classes. **Six contain only our formats. Two contain only other
people's. One contains both** — at 8 bits, `GF+8` 0.1004 against `fp8 e4m3`
0.1254: **we lose by 19.9%**. That row's conformance column reads *no
reference*: the loss and the missing oracle are the same debt.

Nearest honest comparison, 17 bits against 16:

| **BNF16** (17b) versus | |
|---|---|
| `binary16` (16b) | **−3.1%** |
| `takum16` (16b) | **+57.4%** |
| `LNS16` (16b) | **+79.8%** |
| `posit16` (16b) | **+113.4%** |

**Against every research format at comparable width — takum, LNS, posit, the
formats this paper is actually arguing with — our 17-bit design wins by half to
double.** Against IEEE half it loses by three percent. Against IEEE single it
cannot win: that decoder is a wire, and no decoder beats no decoder.

That is the defensible claim. **First place overall belongs to a two-bit format
with no same-width competitor in the catalogue, and second place to an identity
function.** Neither was visible while the table was sorted on one number.

## A mistake made and undone

The solo-harness files were written as `d_<format>.v` and **overwrote a tracked
`fpga/tnet/d_gfternary.v`**. Caught by the harness ratchet: a known
partial-observation entry stopped reproducing, which is normally good news and
this time meant a file had been clobbered. Restored from git, harnesses renamed
`solo_*`, 66 LUT reproduced under the safe name.

*Before overwriting, look at the target.* The ratchet caught it only because it
tracks known-bad entries and notices when one disappears — a gate that only
watched for new failures would have said nothing.
