# T7 applied: which of our comparisons are well-posed

T7 said a quantity depending on an unstated choice has no limit. It cost six
iterations to discover on the LNS ratio. Applied deliberately to the remaining
claims, it partitions them in one pass.

## The accuracy axis: half well-posed, half not

`M_eff` was measured for each format over **its own range**, capped at 120
binades. Different participants were therefore measured on different domains --
the signature of a T7 problem.

**For fixed fields it does not matter.** The precision law says the binade
exponent cancels, so `M_eff` should be domain-independent. Measured across a
14-fold change of domain:

| format | domain ±8 | ±40 | ±110 | spread |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| TNF16 `Et=4` | 11.084 | 11.131 | 11.080 | **0.051** |
| TNF16 `Et=6` | 9.026 | 9.035 | 9.132 | **0.106** |
| TNF16 `Et=3` | 12.056 | 12.093 | 12.095 | **0.039** |

Hundredths of a bit. The law holds and the comparison is well-posed.

**For a taper it matters by construction**, since its precision varies with the
binade. There is no neutral domain: any choice favours one family. We knew the
symptom -- posit16 exceeds the uniform budget when scored where it concentrates
precision -- but had not named it a problem of *posing*.

**So the 28-format frontier mixes two different cases.** Fourteen catalogued
formats are fixed and compared soundly; ten are tapered and **42% of the claim
needs restating**.

## The well-posed replacement

For a fixed field against a taper the correct answer is not who wins but
**where** each does -- the crossover binade, which names the domain instead of
hiding it.

posit16 at `es=2` loses `2^-es = 0.25` bits per binade from unity, starting near
12 bits. TNF16 `Et=6` holds 9.03 everywhere. They meet at:

```
|e| = (12.00 − 9.03) / 0.25 ≈ 12 binades
```

| | verdict |
|---|---|
| `\|e\| < 12` | **posit16** is more accurate |
| `\|e\| > 12` | **TNF16** is more accurate, and the gap grows linearly |
| `\|e\| > 112` | posit16 does not represent at all; TNF16 runs to 729 |

That statement does not depend on a choice of domain, because it states the
domain. It replaces "TNF16 dominates posit16" with something a reader cannot
reverse by re-selecting where to measure.

## Theorem

**T8 (a comparison across a varying property must report a crossover, not a
verdict).** If one system's figure of merit is constant in a parameter and
another's varies in it, no single-number comparison is well-posed. The well-posed
form is the parameter value at which they meet, together with the direction of
the inequality on each side.

**Corollary.** A domination claim between such systems is not made true by
choosing a domain; it is made *meaningless*. The frontier's fixed-against-fixed
rows survive unchanged and its fixed-against-tapered rows are restated as
crossovers.

## Method note

T7 was learned expensively -- six instrument corrections chasing a number that
could not converge. Applied on purpose it took one iteration to partition a
28-row claim into 14 sound rows and 10 that needed a different form. The check is
mechanical: **ask what property is held equal, then ask whether a different
choice would change the answer.** If it would, the single number is not a result.
