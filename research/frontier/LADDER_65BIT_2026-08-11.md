# The 65-bit rebuild does not repeat the 17-bit win, and the reason matters

At 17 bits, moving the rung from $E_t=4$ to $E_t=5$ gave **both** less waste and
more throughput: 36.7% → 5.1% waste, 0.1173 → 0.1312 MHz/LUT, *and* the new rung
guards its offset field while the old one does not.

At 65 bits the same rule sends $E_t=7$ (46.6% waste, the worst in range) to
$E_t=8$ (19.9%). It does not repeat the win.

## One variable at a time

The first comparison was not like for like — the old rung is unguarded and the
new one is guarded, two changes at once. So the unguarded twin was built:

| 65-bit rung | LUT | MHz | MHz/LUT |
|---|---|---|---|
| TNF64, $E_t{=}7$, unguarded | 572 | 65.38 | 0.1143 |
| TNF64b, $E_t{=}8$, **unguarded** | 566 | 64.17 | 0.1134 |
| TNF64b, $E_t{=}8$, guarded | 565 | 56.92 | 0.1007 |

- **the $k$ change alone: −0.8%**
- **the guard alone, at $E_t=8$: −11.2%**

## The −0.8% is not a result

The unguarded twin's five seeds spread 62.88 to 70.53 MHz — **±6% about its own
median.** A −0.8% difference sits far inside that. The honest statement is that
**at 65 bits the choice of $k$ between 7 and 8 is not distinguishable by this
harness**, not that $E_t=8$ is slightly worse.

That is a caution about the 17-bit result too. There the measured gain was
+11.9%, which is larger than the seed spread, but the 17-bit rebuild was never
decomposed either: its +11.9% is the sum of a $k$ change and a guard, and the
guard is known to cost between 1.8% and 15.1% depending on the rung. **The
decomposition at 17 bits has not been done and should be.**

## What survives

Waste $w(k)$ is a property of the format and is exactly known: 46.6% → 19.9% is
real and is not a measurement. Whether that buys throughput is a separate
question, and at 65 bits the answer so far is: not measurably.

**Reducing waste is worth doing for what waste is — codes that name nothing —
not for a throughput gain this harness cannot see.**
