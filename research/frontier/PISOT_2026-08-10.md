# The multiply-free scales are Pisot numbers -- some of them

A Pisot--Vijayaraghavan number is a real algebraic integer greater than one all
of whose conjugates lie strictly inside the unit circle. Our scales are real
algebraic integers with coefficients in {0,±1}, so the question is immediate and
we had not asked it.

| scale | polynomial | r | max conjugate modulus | Pisot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `phi` | r^2 = r + 1 | 1.618034 | 0.618034 | **yes** |
| plastic | r^3 = r + 1 | 1.324718 | 0.868837 | **yes** |
| supergolden | r^3 = r^2 + 1 | 1.465571 | 0.826031 | **yes** |
| tribonacci | r^3 = r^2 + r + 1 | 1.839287 | 0.737353 | **yes** |
| degree-4 minimum | r^4 = -r^3+r^2+r+1 | 1.178724 | **1.512876** | no |
| one-adder, d=4 | r^4 = r + 1 | 1.220744 | **1.063337** | no |
| one-adder, d=5 | r^5 = r^3 + 1 | 1.236506 | **1.050367** | no |
| one-adder, d=8 | r^8 = r + 1 | 1.096982 | **1.083744** | no |

**The one-adder family is Pisot only at d = 2 and d = 3.** Every member from
degree four upward has a conjugate outside the unit circle. That is a fact about
the numbers and it was worth knowing: the two members our datapath actually uses
are exactly the Pisot ones, and we had reached them without knowing why they
were special.

## A prediction of ours that the measurement did not support

Having found the classification, the obvious hardware consequence seemed to be
register growth: a conjugate of modulus greater than one should amplify the
coordinate vector faster than the value grows, so a non-Pisot scale should cost
width.

Measured -- iterating the companion map sixty times from the coordinate vector
of the value one, and comparing the largest coordinate against `r^k`:

| scale | max conjugate | coords at k=60 | r^60 | bits/step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `phi` | 0.6180 | 1.548e+12 | 3.461e+12 | 0.6942 |
| r^8 = r+1 | 1.0837 | 5.600e+01 | 2.582e+02 | 0.1335 |

The coordinates track the value in both cases and the non-Pisot member is no
worse. **The prediction is withdrawn.** In hindsight the reason is plain: the
companion map is exact integer arithmetic iterated from an integer start, and
the dominant eigenvalue is `r`. There is nothing in the conjugate direction to
amplify, because nothing is ever perturbed into it.

Where the Pisot property should matter is the other direction -- ENCODING a real
number into coordinates, where the classical results live: in a Pisot base the
beta-expansion of every element of Q(beta) is eventually periodic, and the
numbers with finite expansion are characterised. That is the place to look, and
this note records that we have not looked there yet rather than implying the
question is closed.

## What is confirmed independently

The classification itself is a computation, not a citation, and it reproduces:
every conjugate modulus above was obtained from `numpy.roots` on the integer
coefficient vector. The two classical facts it should be checked against --
that the plastic number is the smallest Pisot number (Siegel) and that `phi` is
the smallest limit point of the Pisot set (Salem) -- are consistent with our
degree-2 and degree-3 enumerations finding nothing below them, and a literature
check is in progress.
