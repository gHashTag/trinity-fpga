# Closing the 64-bit gap: the scan is superlinear and the recommendation flips

The selection table had three blanks. Two are now filled, and the larger one
settles a row that had no answer.

## What was missing and why

No TNF32 or TNF64 decoder existed in the tree -- only TNF16 -- so the 32- and
64-bit rows had accuracy figures with no decode cost to set against them. Both
are written here following `tnf16_decode`'s layout and respecting the packing
constraint `3^Et * 2^M <= 2^(N-1)`: TNF32 is a 20-bit offset holding 12 trits
plus an 11-bit mantissa, TNF64 a 39-bit offset holding 24 trits plus 24 bits.
Both emit fp32 like every other decoder here, so the harness is identical.

| **64** | **2629** | **5.09x** | **6.78** | **128** | **416.67** | **20.5x** | **61.5x** |

`posit64` is the most expensive decoder in the entire comparison, by a factor of
five over the next.

## Theorem

**T11 (the scan is superlinear in width).** A taper's decode contains a scan of
length `O(N)`, and its area grows as `N^1.16` measured across four widths, while
reading fixed fields grows as `N^0.17` -- effectively flat, because fields are
read by wires regardless of width.

From 32 to 64 bits posit gains **5.09x** in area and loses **7.2x** in frequency.
TNF gains **1.23x** and loses no frequency at all: 416.67 MHz against 391.08.

## The row that had no answer

posit64 leads on accuracy by **6.1 bits** at `|e|=1` and holds a lead to 54
binades, which covers every workload measured in this work. Its decode costs
**20.5x the area and 61.5x the delay**.

That is **0.30 bits of accuracy per multiple of area**. No plausible scarcity in
a datapath makes that exchange favourable, so the 64-bit row is now:

> **TNF64**, and the price is named: 6.1 bits of accuracy given up, 20.5x area
> and 61.5x delay saved.

This is the first row in the selection table where the two axes disagree and the
disagreement is nevertheless decisive. At 16 bits the same trade is 1.76 bits
against 3.0x area, which is genuinely a judgement call; at 64 bits it is not.

**Corollary.** Because the scan is superlinear and field reading is flat, the
trade between a taper and a fixed field is not a constant -- it degrades for the
taper as width grows, and there exists a width beyond which no accuracy advantage
justifies it. Measured here, that width lies between 16 and 64 bits.

## What is still blank

Gradient span is still estimated from the literature rather than measured on a
training run. That is the last blank in the selection table, and it affects only
which row applies, not the numbers in it.
