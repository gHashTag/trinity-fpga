# Two of the three "optima" were never identified by their own data

`flatness.py`, from the stored sweeps only — no model loaded, which matters because the scratchpad
was cleaned overnight and four of five checkpoints are gone.

## The test

The campaign recorded "the optima are flat" and "u\* does not transfer" as two lessons. If the
first causes the second they are one. The discriminating quantity is **depth against that model's
own measured tie-rule floor**: an argmin shallower than the noise it sits in is not an argmin.

| model | dtype | tie floor | u\* | ppl(u\*) | depth | depth/floor | indistinct. region | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pythia | fp16 | 0.5358 | 0.40 | 44.5762 | 0.2457 | **0.46** | 0.07 (12 %) | **undetermined** |
| opt | fp16 | 0.0667 | 0.25 | 31.0971 | 0.0087 | **0.13** | 0.10 (18 %) | **undetermined** |
| gpt2 | fp32 | 0.0003 | 0.25 | 35.6968 | 0.5127 | **1709** | 0.00 (0 %) | **SHARP** |

**Two of three reported optima are shallower than their own noise floor.** Pythia's minimum sits
0.246 below the next alignment while its rounding convention alone moves it 0.536; OPT's sits
0.009 below while its floor is 0.067. Neither model's data identifies a location.

So the headline "u\* spans 0.25–0.40 across five families" is **partly an artefact of reporting an
argmin that two of the three models do not possess.** That does not resurrect the law — the
leave-one-out failure stands, and a transfer that costs Pythia 1.22–1.42 ppl is still a transfer
that costs — but it changes what the spread *is*. It is not five measurements disagreeing; it is
one measurement and several coin flips.

## The caveat that prevents this from being a confirmation

**The sharp subset has one member.** "The sharp models agree at 0.25" is vacuous at n = 1, and I
will not report it as agreement.

**Worse, sharpness is confounded with the release dtype.** GPT-2 is the sharp one *because* it
ships in fp32, whose tie floor is 0.0003 — three orders of magnitude below the fp16 and bf16
checkpoints (skill §4b: floor ≈ 2⁻ᵐ in the stored mantissa width). So the split is not
"models with real optima versus models without"; it is, at least in part, **"the one checkpoint
whose release format lets an optimum be resolved at all, versus four where the floor swamps it."**

That is the sharper statement, and it is a claim about measurement rather than about quantisation:

> **A fine alignment optimum can only be resolved on an fp32-released checkpoint. On bf16 and
> fp16 releases the rounding-convention floor is larger than the effect, so any argmin reported
> from them is noise — regardless of whether an optimum exists.**

Testing it needs an fp32 and a bf16 release of the *same* model, which is the obvious next
experiment and which the current checkpoint set cannot supply.

## Consequence

- The two campaign lessons ("flat optima", "fitted constants do not transfer") are **plausibly one
  mechanism**, not confirmed to be. Recorded as a hypothesis with its falsifier, not as a finding.
- Any future alignment sweep must report **depth against the model's own floor** alongside u\*.
  An argmin without that ratio is uninterpretable, and three of this campaign's were.
- `flatness.py` also cost a small lesson of its own: its first loader **guessed three JSON schemas
  and found none**, printing "no stored sweep on disk" for files that were sitting there. Reading
  the schema took one command. Same class as the log search that failed on guessed filenames.

# Fourth model added: the hypothesis is refuted, and so is my repair of it

Extending the loader to the second stored schema (`scale_settled_*.json` keeps the alignment
CONSTANT `c`, not `u`; converting by `u = 1 − log_g(max_norm/c)`, checked against the harness's own
two asserted anchors: `c=3 → u=0`, `c=4 → u=0.41504`) brings SmolLM2 into the table.

| model | stored | floor | u\* | depth | depth/floor | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **smollm2** | **bf16** | 0.2398 | **0.341** | 0.5104 | **2.13** | **SHARP** |
| gpt2 | fp32 | 0.0003 | 0.250 | 0.5127 | 1709 | SHARP |
| pythia | fp16 | 0.5358 | 0.400 | 0.2457 | 0.46 | undetermined |
| opt | fp16 | 0.0667 | 0.250 | 0.0087 | 0.13 | undetermined |

## Both claims die, and the pre-registration is why

**1. The unified story is refuted.** `flatness.py` stated before running: *"if the sharp subset
also disagrees, they are two independent problems and the law is dead for a second, separate
reason."* The sharp subset now has two members and **they disagree — 0.341 against 0.250, a spread
of 0.09.** So "the optima are flat" and "u\* does not transfer" are **two problems, not one**. The
undetermined models inflate the spread from 0.09 to 0.15, but removing them does not close it.

**2. My own repair from the previous cycle is refuted.** I wrote that *"a fine alignment optimum is
only resolvable on an fp32-released checkpoint; on bf16 and fp16 the floor swamps it."* **SmolLM2
is bf16, carries a floor 800× higher than GPT-2's, and is resolvable anyway** — because its optimum
is genuinely deeper (0.5104). Resolvability is `depth/floor`, and I had generalised from the one
term I had just discovered. Same error as fitting a constant to two checkpoints, made about a
mechanism rather than a number.

## What actually stands

- **Two of four reported optima are not identified by their own data** (ratios 0.46 and 0.13).
  That part survives and is worth carrying: any alignment argmin must be quoted with its
  depth-against-floor.
- **Among the two that ARE identified, u\* still disagrees by 0.09.** The law is dead on its own
  terms, not merely obscured by noise.
- The dtype→floor mechanism (§4b, floor ≈ 2⁻ᵐ, predicted 8×, measured 7.96) is untouched — it
  explains the *floor*, and never claimed to explain the *depth*.

**Still missing: Qwen.** Its sweep is not on disk in either schema, so the table is four of five.
That is stated rather than papered over, and it is the first thing a rerun should restore.

# What sets the depth? Not the block-maximum distribution. Hypothesis refuted.

`depth_mechanism.py`, all five checkpoints restored and sha256-verified against the HF API digest.

**The hypothesis**, stated with its falsifier before running: alignment slides a window over the
block maxima, so what it can achieve depends on how those maxima sit inside their binades. Write
`a = 2^e · 2^f` with `f = frac(log2 a)`. If `f` is uniform, moving the window trades one clamped
set for an equally numerous one — flat optimum. If `f` is concentrated, some placements dodge the
mass — deep optimum. So depth should track the **non-uniformity of `f`**, measurably, from the
weights alone with no perplexity run.

| model | layers | blocks | KS from uniform | TV from uniform | depth | depth/floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| smollm2 | 210 | 3,317,760 | 0.0590 | 0.0966 | 0.5104 | 2.13 |
| qwen | 168 | 11,182,080 | 0.0351 | 0.1096 | — | — |
| pythia | 48 | 2,654,208 | 0.0085 | 0.0160 | 0.2457 | 0.46 |
| opt | 72 | 2,654,208 | 0.0483 | 0.0486 | 0.0087 | 0.13 |
| gpt2 | 48 | 2,654,208 | 0.0148 | 0.0149 | 0.5127 | 1709 |

    Spearman(KS, depth) = +0.000      Pearson = -0.084
    Spearman(TV, depth) = -0.400      Pearson = +0.181

    order by non-uniformity:  smollm2, opt, gpt2, pythia
    order by depth:           gpt2, smollm2, pythia, opt

**Refuted, and not marginally.** The rank correlation is exactly zero on KS and the wrong sign on
TV. OPT has the second-highest non-uniformity and the *lowest* depth (0.0087); GPT-2 has low
non-uniformity and the *highest*. The orderings share no structure.

## What that leaves

Depth is **not a property of the weight distribution**. Every parameter-free statistic of the
block maxima tried so far — clamp fraction, kurtosis, `frac(log2)` non-uniformity — fails to order
it. That is the same wall this campaign hit twice already:

* per-layer weight MSE correlates with per-layer perplexity damage at **r = +0.13** on two models;
* no weight statistic separated the three families whose 4-bit ladder winner differs;
* and now no weight statistic orders the depth of the alignment optimum.

The consistent reading is that **these quantities are properties of the model's loss response, not
of its weights.** Weight statistics predict weight-space error and stop there. Anything asking
"how much does this hurt the model" has, in this campaign, required measuring the model.

If that is right it is a limit on the whole enterprise of picking formats from weight histograms,
and it has now been reached from three independent directions.

**n = 4.** A rank correlation on four points is weak, and I am reporting the ordering rather than
the coefficient for that reason. But a hypothesis that predicts an ordering and gets a completely
different one is refuted regardless of n.

**Qwen is now measurable.** Its checkpoint is restored; its KS and TV are in the table above, and
only the depth is missing because its sweep was lost. One u-sweep closes the table to five.
