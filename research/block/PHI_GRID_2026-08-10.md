# The phi grid is twice as good as powers of two, at the same hardware cost

## The gap this closes

The `{-φ, 0, +φ}` alphabet had a proof (exactness in `Z[φ]`, machine-checked)
and a silicon number (0.177 MHz/LUT), but no answer to the practical question a
reviewer asks first: **BitNet stores ternary weights plus a real per-layer scale
`α = mean|W|`, and multiplying by that α puts the multiplier straight back. What
does it cost to remove it?**

Three grids for the layer scale, all at 2 bits per weight:

| grid | spacing | hardware cost |
|---|---|---|
| exact `α*` (BitNet) | continuous | **a multiplier** |
| `φ^k` (ours) | `log₂φ` = 0.694 octave | shift-and-add, exact by `Z[φ]` closure |
| `2^k` | 1 octave | shift |

The φ grid is denser by `log(2)/log(φ) = 1.440` at the same cost class.
**Prediction made before measuring:** the excess error of `φ^k` over the
unreachable exact `α*` should be about **half** that of `2^k`.

## Measured, 210 linear layers of SmolLM2-135M

`α*` is the least-squares optimum for fixed ternary support, so it is the
smallest of the three by construction — asserted as a ruler check before reading
anything else.

| grid | mean rel. error | excess over exact |
|---|---:|---:|
| exact `α*` | 0.476781 | 0.0000% |
| **`φ^k`** | **0.488424** | **2.4420%** |
| `2^k` | 0.499943 | 4.8579% |

**Ratio of excesses: 0.501 against a predicted 0.500.**

Per-layer medians: φ 1.4198%, 2^k 2.9604%. φ beats `2^k` in **163 of 210**
layers — not all, because when `α*` happens to land near a power of two the
coarser grid wins that layer. The average and the ratio hold regardless.

## What this is and is not

**Is:** the layer scale can be removed from the multiplier path at half the
accuracy cost of the obvious alternative, and applied exactly rather than
approximately. Together with `dot_exact` this closes the multiplier out of the
whole layer — weights, accumulation, and now the scale.

**Is not:** a model-quality result. It is reconstruction error on real weights.

## Self-caught defect #13: three arms inside a destroyed regime

The first attempt asked this through perplexity. Post-hoc ternarisation destroys
a model that was not trained for it, so **every** arm — including BitNet's exact
α — landed at ppl ≈ 2.2e7 against a baseline of 14.49:

| arm | ppl |
|---|---:|
| fp32 baseline | 14.4874 |
| exact α (BitNet) | 22,123,477 |
| `φ^k` | 65,839,457 |
| `2^k` | 31,616,709 |

Read naively this says "φ is refuted, and by 2×". It says nothing: three points
inside a collapsed regime are not a comparison, and the ordering there is noise.
The tell was that the *control* arm — the one that should have been near-lossless
— was destroyed too. **A comparison whose control fails is not a comparison.**

Replaced with the quantity the question is actually about. The proper perplexity
test needs quantisation-aware training, which is a separate piece of work.
